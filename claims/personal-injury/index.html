Could you claim?

Could your circumstances support a claim?

  • You were hurt in an accident that was not your fault, or not only your fault
  • Someone else should have kept you safe: another driver, your employer, or whoever runs the place where it happened
  • It happened in England or Wales
  • It happened in the last three years, or you only recently learned the injury came from it

You may still be able to claim if you were partly responsible, although compensation is usually reduced by your share of the blame. A claim expert will assess your circumstances.

After an accident

Five practical steps after an accident

You can contact us before gathering these details. Where possible, the following steps can help record what happened.

  1. 01

    Get medical help

    Seek medical attention for your injury and keep a record of appointments and treatment.

  2. 02

    Report it

    Report the accident to your employer or the venue. After a road accident, contact the police where required.

  3. 03

    Take photographs

    Where safe, photograph the location, the cause of the accident and your injuries as soon as possible.

  4. 04

    Note who saw it

    Record the names and contact details of anyone who witnessed the accident.

  5. 05

    Keep your receipts

    Keep receipts for travel, medication and treatment, along with records of any lost earnings.

Compensation

What compensation can cover

For the injury itself

  • The pain and suffering it caused
  • The effect on your everyday life, your work and your hobbies

For what it has cost you

  • Lost earnings, now and in the future
  • Medical treatment and physiotherapy
  • Care and help at home
  • Travel to appointments
  • Changes to your home or car

Compensation depends on the circumstances of your claim. Your solicitor assesses the injury, its effects and the losses supported by evidence.

Time limits

Give yourself time to understand your options

In England and Wales, a personal injury claim usually has to be started within three years of the accident, or of the day you first knew the injury came from it. For a child, the three years start on their 18th birthday.

Preparing a claim takes time. Contact us early so your circumstances can be assessed before the relevant deadline.

Your personal injury questions answered

How long does a personal injury claim take?

It depends on the injury, on whether the other side accepts the blame and on whether the claim goes to court. A serious injury takes longer, because its full effect has to be known before the claim can be valued.

Can I claim if I was partly responsible?

Often, yes. Your compensation is reduced by your share of the blame. This is called contributory negligence, and your solicitor will explain how it applies to you.

Do I have to use a claims management company?

No. You can make a claim yourself or go to a solicitor directly. For a minor injury in a road accident, as a driver or a passenger, you can also use the government's free Official Injury Claim service. Most claims companies are not solicitors and cannot represent you in court: Claim 3000 introduces you to a solicitor who can.

What if the driver was uninsured or cannot be traced?

The Motor Insurers' Bureau compensates people injured by uninsured drivers and by drivers who cannot be traced. A claim expert can tell you how it works.

What if I was injured in a violent crime?

Injuries from violent crime are claimed through a separate government scheme, run by the Criminal Injuries Compensation Authority.
